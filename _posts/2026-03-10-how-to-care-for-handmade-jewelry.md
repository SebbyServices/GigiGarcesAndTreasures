Handmade jewelry is different from the stuff you find at a department store chain. It's made with natural materials, hand-finished clasps, and genuine stones — which means it responds to the world around it in ways that mass-produced jewelry doesn't.

That's part of what makes it beautiful. It's also part of why it needs a little more attention.

Here's exactly how I recommend caring for your GigiG pieces.

## The Golden Rule: Take It Off

Before you shower, swim, exercise, do dishes, or apply lotion or perfume — take your jewelry off first. Water and chemicals are the two biggest enemies of natural stone and metal hardware. Even a quick rinse in the shower can dull stones over time.

I wear mine everywhere, but I built the habit of always removing jewelry before water and always putting it on after perfume has dried. It becomes second nature quickly.

## Storing Your Pieces

The best storage for handmade jewelry is a soft pouch or a small jewelry box with individual compartments. You want each piece to have its own space so stones don't scratch each other and clasps don't tangle with chains.

If you don't have a jewelry box, a small ziplock bag or a folded cloth will work in a pinch. The goal is just to keep things separated and away from dust and humidity.

Avoid leaving jewelry on a bathroom counter — the humidity from showers accelerates tarnish on metal and can dull some stone finishes.

## Cleaning Your Pieces

For most GigiG pieces, a soft dry cloth after wearing is all you need. Gently wipe the metal accents and the surface of the stones to remove any oils from skin contact.

For pieces that need a bit more attention:
- Use a barely damp cloth (water only, no soap) on stone beads
- Dry immediately and completely
- Never soak handmade jewelry
- Never use ultrasonic cleaners — they can crack stones and loosen adhesives

## When Something Goes Wrong

If a clasp feels loose, a wire looks bent, or a bead seems like it might be sliding — stop wearing that piece and contact me. I would rather repair a piece before it breaks than after. Most repairs are simple and free; I just need you to reach out before the piece fails completely.

If your order arrived damaged, please contact me within 48 hours with photos. I will make it right.

Caring for handmade jewelry is really just caring for the materials it's made from — natural stone, genuine metal, real workmanship. Treat it gently and it will last for years.
